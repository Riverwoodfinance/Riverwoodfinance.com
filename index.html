01 / EU regulation & oversight

Three pages.
A much bigger obligation.

Before a covered product is made available to retail investors, its manufacturer must prepare and publish a KID. The obligation reaches beyond the document to its calculations, review and supporting procedures.[1][2]

Legal basis: PRIIPs Regulation, Articles 22–29 (opens in a new tab). Measures depend on the circumstances and applicable rules; there is no standard penalty for every KID error.

Riverwood’s response: bring production, calculation evidence and ongoing control into one repeatable process. Better documentation, not a guarantee against supervisory findings.

Deterministic regulatory production

Versioned rules produce the figures.

Regulatory calculations, method selection, normalisation and release criteria must be reproducible and auditable. The same approved inputs and rule version must give the same result.

Normal production without manual specialist handling.

Riverwood is being designed for straight-through production within validated method families. Human expertise develops and maintains the methods, validates the system, performs sample checks and resolves system-generated exceptions. Customers still confirm their inputs. Critical data gaps or an unsupported method must stop the normal flow.

Controlled customer access

Role-based access, least privilege, encryption and separate development, test and production environments are architecture requirements.

Private inputs stay separate

Client inputs and calculations must not automatically become shared peer data or model-training material. Each use needs its own authorisation and purpose.

Independent checks before release

Reference calculations, validated test cases and regression tests support release gates. Critical failures block release; corrections retain a traceable history.

These are planned controls, to be validated before production. Riverwood does not claim regulatory approval or ISO/SOC certification. The product manufacturer’s regulatory responsibility remains in place.

What is included, and what is agreed separately?

What counts as a profile?

A distinct calculation basis for a fund or share class, defined by the inputs and methodology. A language version or a new file is not automatically a new calculation profile.

New production and lifecycle

A new KID can be purchased on its own. At the indicative prices, new production plus one year of lifecycle totals EUR 3,500. The portfolio fee covers lifecycle, not unlimited new production.

Document review or recalculation?

A document review can assess visible content and consistency. Method verification or recalculation requires the relevant underlying inputs. A review is not an audit opinion or an assurance guarantee.

Migration, changes and responsibility

Migration reviews, language requirements, material changes, publication and any supplementary deliverables are scoped explicitly. The same control is not charged twice as both included lifecycle and a separate review.

Lifecycle / The recurring obligation

The next review
doesn’t start from zero.

PRIIPs review rules require an assessment at least every 12 months and when relevant changes arise. A review and a new document are different events.[3]

A control result has value,
even when the figures stay the same.

The planned lifecycle process preserves the profile, evidence and review history, whether a change requires recalculation or confirms the KID remains current.

Review guidance: CSSF, 28 May 2024 (opens in a new tab) · Danish FSA (opens in a new tab).

    Establish the basis.

    Validate the supported profile, inputs and initial control evidence.

  2. 02 / MONITOR

    Identify the next trigger.

    Track relevant input and rule changes, review dates and agreed thresholds.

  3. 03 / REVIEW

    Reassess. Recalculate when needed.

Before we start

A few important
distinctions.

Clear scope is part of a credible production process.

Is Riverwood available for full production today?

Riverwood is under development. Conversations about scoped reviews, paid pilots and future production are welcome. A pilot or limited review is not the same as a fully launched straight-through platform. Each engagement depends on agreed scope, method fit and readiness.

Does every investment fund need a KID?

No. The requirement depends on the product and its investor and distribution scope, not simply a manager’s registration. Riverwood’s initial service scope will also depend on supported methods and the necessary inputs. Read the Danish FSA guidance (opens in a new tab).

Can a review confirm the underlying calculation?

Not from a PDF alone. A document review can assess the visible information and consistency. Checking a method or reproducing a calculation requires the relevant source data, assumptions and method-specific inputs. The agreed evidence level and any untested areas must be explicit.

Who remains responsible for the KID?

The product manufacturer retains its regulatory responsibility. Input confirmation, publication, correction and service responsibilities must be agreed explicitly. Riverwood does not offer a guarantee of regulatory approval or freedom from supervisory findings. PRIIPs Regulation (opens in a new tab).

Will confidential inputs be used for shared peer data?

Not automatically. Riverwood’s design separates private client inputs and calculations from common market and peer data. Any shared use or model-training use needs its own authorised basis and purpose. A public KID and its private calculation inputs are different information layers.

What should we bring to the first conversation?

Your organisation, product type, distribution country, approximate number of profiles and the next KID or review deadline. A public KID link can help define the task. Do not send confidential inputs before an appropriate data-sharing arrangement has been agreed.
