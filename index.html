The engine behind Europe's key information documents

Every investment fund marketed to retail or semi-professional investors in the European Union must publish a key information document setting out its costs, its risk and its performance scenarios — in the official language of each country where it is sold, reviewed every year.

This scale appears in every key information document in Europe. Behind it sit a volatility-based risk measure, a credit assessment, four performance scenarios and a full cost disclosure — each governed by prescribed method.

The requirement

It follows the sale, not the fund

The obligation attaches to where a fund is marketed, not where it is established. A Luxembourg fund sold to Danish investors needs a Danish document. A US sponsor raising European capital needs one in every market it approaches.

The engine

Repeatable, and traceable

A versioned calculation framework for the risk indicator, cost disclosure and performance scenarios, coupled with a controlled production pipeline. Every figure traces to its source; every document to the model version that produced it.

The data

A record of what funds disclose

Published key information documents are public. Structured together, they show what European funds actually charge, the risk they report and the holding periods they recommend — the basis for peer comparison.
